Serving as the custodian of Karnataka's historical treasures, epigraphy, and artistic expressions since the 19th century.

Institutional Profile

A Sanctum of History & Culture

Karnataka Heritage is one of South India's oldest public museum institutions, committed to the comprehensive study, conservation, and exhibition of the historical heritage of the Deccan plateau.

Housed in an architecturally celebrated neoclassical red-brick edifice overlooking Cubbon Park, the museum preserves antiquities unearthed from historic excavation sites across all 31 districts of Karnataka—from Chandravalli and Sannati to Hampi and Talakadu.

Our galleries serve as a vital link connecting modern citizens and scholars with the artistic triumphs of dynasties that shaped peninsular India—including the Kadambas, Badami Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Hoysalas, the Vijayanagara Empire, and the Wodeyars of Mysore.

Our Origins

Our History & Founding

Pioneering Archaeological Beginnings

The museum's origins date back to 1865 when surgeon and naturalist Dr. Edward Balfour recommended establishing a repository in Bangalore for geological specimens, stone inscriptions, and ethnological records of the Mysore Princely State.

In 1877, under the patronage of Maharaja Chamarajendra Wodeyar X and British Chief Commissioner Sir Richard Meade, the present neoclassical heritage structure was formally inaugurated. The legendary epigraphist Benjamin Lewis Rice subsequently enriched the collection with hundreds of stone and copper-plate inscriptions that formed the bedrock of his monumental 12-volume survey, Epigraphia Carnatica.

20th-Century Expansion & Modernization

Under pioneering Indian archaeologist Dr. M. H. Krishna in the 1930s, the museum underwent major taxonomic reorganization. Systematic archaeological digs at Chandravalli yielded rare Roman coins, proving maritime trade links with the Mediterranean dating back to the 1st century BCE.

Following Indian Independence and the linguistic reorganization of Karnataka in 1956, the institution became the principal State Museum repository, expanding its conservation wings, digital archive, and educational outreach to schools across rural and urban Karnataka.

🎯 Our Mission

To collect, safeguard, research, and interpret the tangible and intangible heritage of Karnataka; to foster historical empathy and civic pride among people of all backgrounds through inspiring exhibitions, scholastic publications, and inclusive community engagement.

  • Conduct scholarly fieldwork and support rescue archaeology
  • Maintain world-class chemical and physical conservation labs
  • Make classical epigraphy accessible through open digital repositories
  • Partner with educational institutions to mentor future historians

👁️ Our Vision

To be recognized globally as an authoritative center of excellence for Deccan history and peninsular Indian art, where ancient heritage sparks contemporary dialogue, scientific inquiry, and cultural pride for generations to come.

  • 100% digitization and open-access metadata for all 15,000 antiquities
  • Accessible and barrier-free galleries for differently abled visitors
  • Active preservation of endangered indigenous craft techniques
  • Collaboration with international museums for exchange exhibitions
Behind the Scenes

Cultural Preservation & Conservation Work

Antiquities are fragile witnesses to time. Our dedicated laboratory ensures artifacts survive atmospheric pollution, humidity, and biological decay.

🔬 Stone & Sculptural Conservation

Karnataka's soapstone and sandstone artifacts are treated for salt efflorescence, micro-flaking, and biological lichen growth using non-destructive ultrasonic and chemical poulticing techniques.

📜 Palm-Leaf & Paper Manuscript Preservation

Over 2,200 palm-leaf manuscripts in Kannada, Sanskrit, and Prakrit are cleaned with natural citronella oils, fumigated against insect infestations, and digitized in high-resolution RAW archival formats.

🛡️ Metallurgical & Wootz Steel Care

Antique armaments, copper-plates, and panchaloha sacred bronzes undergo electrochemical stabilization, corrosion inhibition, and micro-crystalline waxing to seal surfaces from ambient moisture.

Governance

Museum Administration & Curatorial Board

Karnataka Heritage functions under the administrative oversight of the Department of Archaeology, Museums and Heritage, Government of Karnataka, guided by an independent Academic Advisory Council comprising distinguished university professors, archaeologists, and conservation specialists.

Curatorial Directorate

Oversees collection acquisitions, authentication, and exhibition narratives across classical and folk galleries.

Epigraphical Research Division

Responsible for the transcription, linguistic analysis, and cataloguing of stone inscriptions and copper-plates.

Education & Community Outreach

Designs school curricula, conducts heritage walks, organizes teachers' training seminars, and manages docent programs.

Archives & Digital Assets Lab

Maintains high-fidelity photographic records, 3D structural scans, and public research documentation.

Why Heritage Preservation Matters

Heritage is not merely a collection of silent stones or decayed manuscripts; it is the constitutional memory of who we are. Karnataka's monuments embody centuries of engineering ingenuity, ecological wisdom, philosophical pluralism, and artistic discipline. Preserving them protects the cultural identity of our society against rapid erasure, while inspiring future generations of architects, artists, and citizens with the triumphs of human craftsmanship.
