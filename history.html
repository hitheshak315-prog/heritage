History & Heritage of Karnataka

An unbroken chronicle of five millennia of human civilization, dynastic achievements, architectural wonders, and cultural triumphs.

Chronological Survey

The Eight Great Epochs

From early prehistoric dolmens and Ashokan rock edicts to the jewel-encrusted thrones of Mysore and the modern linguistic state.

Prehistory to c. 300 CE

1. Ancient Karnataka & Mauryan Edicts

Prehistoric rock shelters at Kupgal and megalithic dolmens at Brahmagiri reveal flourishing Neolithic cultures. During the 3rd century BCE, Emperor Ashoka left major rock edicts at Maski, Brahmagiri, and Sannati. The Maski inscription is momentous because it was the first edict where Emperor Ashoka was explicitly named as Devanampriya Ashoka.

Key Highlights: Ashokan Brahmi script edicts at Sannati (Bhima river), Shatavahana coins at Chandravalli, Roman gold coins testifying to active spice trade.
c. 345 – 525 CE

2. The Kadambas of Banavasi

Founded by the scholar-warrior Mayurasharma, the Kadamba dynasty established the first indigenous sovereign kingdom of Karnataka with its capital at Banavasi (Uttara Kannada). They were contemporaries of the Western Gangas of Talakadu. The Kadambas made Kannada an administrative language alongside Sanskrit.

Key Highlights: The celebrated Halmidi Inscription (c. 450 CE), Madhukeshwara Temple at Banavasi, Kadamba script that evolved into modern Kannada characters.
c. 543 – 753 CE & 973 – 1198 CE

3. The Chalukyas of Badami & Kalyana

The Badami Chalukyas, led by emperor Pulakeshin II, united the entire Deccan and defeated Northern Emperor Harsha on the banks of the Narmada in 618 CE. They transformed the Malaprabha river valley (Aihole, Badami, Pattadakal) into a legendary open-air laboratory for structural stone temple design, combining Nagara and Dravida architectural styles.

Key Highlights: UNESCO World Heritage Pattadakal group of monuments, Badami rock-cut cave temples, Aihole inscription composed by poet Ravikirti.
c. 1026 – 1343 CE

4. The Hoysala Golden Age

Ruling from Belur and later Dorasamudra (Halebidu), the Hoysalas under King Vishnuvardhana and Queen Shantala Devi raised Indian temple sculpture to an unmatched artistic zenith. Utilizing soft chloritic schist (soapstone), guild masters like Ruvari Mallitamma and Dasoja carved miniature jewelry, mythological friezes, and celestial Madanika figures with microscopic delicacy.

Key Highlights: Chennakesava Temple at Belur (1117 CE), Hoysaleshwara Temple at Halebidu, Keshava Temple at Somanathapura (UNESCO World Heritage sites).
c. 1336 – 1646 CE

5. The Vijayanagara Empire

Founded on the banks of the sacred Tungabhadra river by brothers Harihara I and Bukka Raya I with the guidance of sage Vidyaranya, Vijayanagara grew into one of the largest and most prosperous medieval empires in world history. Under Emperor Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529 CE), Hampi became a magnificent cosmopolitan capital visited by Portuguese, Persian, and Italian travelers.

Key Highlights: UNESCO World Heritage Hampi, Stone Chariot at Vittala Temple, musical granite pillars, Mahanavami Dibba, international diamond and horse bazars.
c. 1399 – 1947 CE

6. The Wodeyars & Kingdom of Mysore

Beginning as feudatories of Vijayanagara, the Yaduraya-descended Wodeyar dynasty established a celebrated kingdom renowned for cultural patronage, statecraft, and public welfare. Mysore saw periods of intense military conflict under Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan, who pioneered rocketry and resisted British colonial armies. Later, under Maharaja Krishnaraja Wodeyar IV, Mysore was hailed as a "Model State" in India.

Key Highlights: World-famous Mysore Dasara festivities, Mysore Palace (Amba Vilas), founding of IISc, University of Mysore, and Asia's first hydroelectric project at Shivanasamudra.
c. 1799 – 1947 CE

7. The Colonial Era & Freedom Struggles

Following the fall of Srirangapatna in 1799, the British East India Company assumed indirect control while restoring the Wodeyar monarchy. Karnataka's people organized fierce resistance: Kittur Rani Chennamma and her lieutenant Sangolli Rayanna led heroic uprisings against the British Doctrine of Lapse in 1824, while Rani Abbakka of Ullal had resisted foreign maritime dominance centuries prior.

Key Highlights: Kittur rebellion (1824), Halagali Bedas uprising (1857), Shivapura flag satyagraha (1938), pioneering epigraphical documentation by Lewis Rice.
1947 – Present

8. Modern Karnataka & Unification

Through the persistent efforts of the Karnataka Ekikarana (Unification) movement led by leaders like Aluru Venkata Rao and Huilgol Narayana Rao, Kannada-speaking regions spread across the Bombay, Madras, and Hyderabad presidencies and Mysore State were unified on November 1, 1956. On November 1, 1973, under Chief Minister D. Devaraj Urs, the state was officially christened Karnataka.

Key Highlights: Formation of Greater Mysore (1956), rechristened Karnataka (1973), leadership in science, aerospace, information technology, and literature with eight Jnanpith Awards.
